# Coaching rules

How to shape Paolo's week. These apply to every plan and every check-in. The coach's plan (plan-structure.md) gives
the week pattern, the workouts and their progressions; where these rules name a change from it, these rules win.

## Standing preferences

They live in Coach (Settings → Coaching preferences) and the profile prints them under **Coaching preferences**.
Follow them as defaults he doesn't need to repeat.

- **Timezone**: America/Los_Angeles, unless the profile's travel section gives another zone for a day.
- **Lift templates** (in the profile) are the strength programming. Don't invent new lifts.
- **Lift notes** (the profile's "Notes from Lift") are Paolo's own words from his last workouts: "Achilles sore",
  "felt heavy", "go up 5 lb". Act on each one in the next days and say how in the strength workout's note: pain or a
  niggle means lighter or swapped work for what it loads (and easy running around it), heavy means hold the load,
  "go up" means raise it. Name the note you are answering, so he sees it was read.
- **Goals**: the milestone goals in the profile ("Goals: milestones") override the inferred goals.
- **Skips**: read "Skipped workouts (last 14 days)". Two or more skips for tired, sore or sick mean a fatigued week
  (say why), starting with 2 easy days. Never carry a skipped workout into a new week to make it up.

## Hard constraints

- **Calendar locks**: existing rides like Bike Club Thu, weddings, travel.
- **Travel**: the profile's travel section gives the place for each day and the travel days. Travel days → rest or
  easy only. Days away from home → no bike or barbell unless a gym is known; prefer running and the hotel dumbbell
  lift. Read the travel section, don't eyeball the calendar: layovers under 18 h are already treated as transit.
- **Form (TSB)**: below −10 → recovery; above +5 → a chance for quality. Below −15 → an unplanned recovery week.
- **Polarized**: about 80 % Z1–Z2, about 20 % Z3+, and the 20 % must be real (VO2, threshold, goal pace such as
  4:00/km for the 10k).

## The shape of the week

- **Weekdays: workout / aerobic / workout / aerobic-recovery / workout.** Up to 3 workouts a week; with fewer free
  days, keep the workouts and drop aerobic days. No back-to-back hard days.
- **Workouts in order**: take them from the profile's "Workout rotation" (Threshold A → VO2 A → VO2 B → Threshold B →
  Threshold A → …). If you deviate, say so and give the reason.
- **Progression**: the rotation gives each bank workout's last prescription and whether it was done. Done → the next
  progression step from the workout bank. Not done → repeat it or step back. Put the exact set (e.g. `7×5 min Z4
  top`) in `intensity`.
- **Bike or run by goal**: each workout can be a ride or a run (the bank has run versions). At least 1 run workout a
  week for the 10k goal; 2 when the 10k goal is behind or within 8 weeks of its deadline. Rides serve VO2 max and the
  ride km goal.
- **10k test**: when the rotation says a test is due, replace one workout with a 10k run at goal pace
  (`run-test-10k`) on a fresh day, with an easy day before it. It does not move the order. About every 6–8 weeks, or
  when a goal looks close: drop volume 20–30 % the days before and add an opener 2 days out.
- **Weekend**: a long ride (2–6 h, mostly Z1/Z2) and a long run (90 min–3 h, mostly Z1 with a solid Z2 part). A long
  ride can hold a workout only if the week was light. If Sunday was long and hard, Monday is aerobic recovery.
  Progress by adding time and time at the top of Z2. Long ride weekends are sacred.
- **Ski touring (Nov–Jun)**: a ski tour (`ski-tour`) can replace the long ride as the weekend long aerobic day. When
  the ski goal needs peaks, offer the weekend to a tour.
- **Aerobic days**: 30–90 min Z1/Z2, with 6 × 6–8 s bike sprints or 4–6 × 20 s run strides, ≥3 min rest between.
- **His rhythm** (365 days in the profile): long ride Sat or Sun morning and the long run the other weekend day; runs
  Tue/Wed/Thu morning; strength on aerobic days, straight after the aerobic part.
- **Place sessions around his meetings, not fixed weekdays.** He isn't working right now, so the calendar decides.
  Put the lifts and the quality sessions in the widest free windows of the week, away from the busiest meeting days,
  and keep a heavy meeting day to one easy session or rest.

## One session a day, or one stacked block

Paolo does a second session only when it follows straight on from the first; a second session hours later gets
dropped (both lifts missed in the week of 2026-10-19 were the PM half of a split day).

- Never plan two sessions hours apart on one day.
- A lift on an aerobic day is one block: the aerobic part first and easy (Z1–low Z2, no strides), the lift straight
  after, as two events with back-to-back times (the first ends when the second starts).
- Good blocks: an easy run to the gym (15–25 min, 10–15 min before `Heavy barbell`; Fitness SF Fillmore is a 10–15
  min walk from home, so take a longer way round), or a Zwift ride, then `Upper (dumbbells)` at home.
- **Never a run after a lift**: he is tight and not in the mood; he walks home.
- When no block fits the day, the lift stands alone and the aerobic session moves or drops.
- Coach enforces this on import (two timed sessions more than 15 min apart are closed into one block; a run after a
  lift is moved ahead of it), so plan the block yourself to get the times you meant.

## Strength

- **2 lifts a week, not 3; 1 is the floor.** Two lifts grow the arms; one holds strength for months. Arm growth is
  the goal running interferes with least, so lifts are not dropped to make room for cardio. Strength is upstream of
  durability.
- The 2 lifts are at least 24 h apart: `Heavy barbell` (lower + pull, at the gym) and `Upper (dumbbells)` at home.
  `Hotel gym (dumbbells)` replaces either one when traveling.
- **Never a heavy lift on the same day as a workout** (Paolo's rule; it overrides the coach's plan). Put the heavy
  lift on an aerobic day; the hotel lift can follow an aerobic day or travel.
- **Short weeks** (travel, newborn): a home mini session from plan-structure.md ("Home mini sessions") keeps the
  floor. Home has 15, 20 and 25 lb dumbbells and bands.
- **Arm top-up at home**: when a lift falls off or the week is a floor week, the "Arm finisher" home mini session can
  follow a Zwift ride as one block.
- **Never miss twice.** The profile's "Consistency and the 4-week block" gives lifts in the last 28 days and the last
  lift. When it says "never miss twice", the week's first lift goes in its first 2 days (40 min is fine).

## Physio every week

Rehab for the right Achilles, knee and hip (Paolo, 2026-10-25).

- Two sessions on a full week, Physio A and Physio B from plan-structure.md ("Physio"), 48 h apart; one (Physio A) on
  a floor, travel or newborn week. On top of the 2 lifts, never instead of one.
- On a rest or easy day alone, or stacked straight after a ride. Not on a lift day, never before a run, and Physio A
  not the day before a run workout or the long run.
- `workoutType` `strength-home`, title starting "🩹 Physio", its numbered lines copied into `description`.
- When a Lift note says the Achilles or knee is sore, keep the physio (it is the treatment) at the last load, and
  make the runs around it easy or a ride.

## Daily back & hip stretch (from 2026-10-26)

Paolo wants more stretching. Until he says to stop (ask him on 2026-11-16):

- The "Back & hip stretch" from plan-structure.md every day, 10 min, as one block straight after the day's session.
  It is not physio: the 2 physio sessions stay, and the plan check does not count it.
- Lifts: back squat and other heavy hinges at RPE 7 at most, 2–3 reps in reserve, no new top weights; anything that
  hurts the back or buttock comes out for a split squat or a machine. Runs and rides as planned when they don't hurt.
- When a Lift note or a check-in says the back or glute is sore, swap the next run for a ride.

## Heat and weather are load

- Compare the destination's temperatures to what he's adapted to (SF is 12–22 °C). A jump of more than 8 °C means he
  is not heat-adapted.
- In heat: start early, cap by HR not pace, and tell him to expect 20–30 s/km slower for the same effort.
- Poor weather at home → Zwift.

## Full weeks, floor weeks and the month

Life stress (sleep, the baby due Feb 2027, travel) fills the same recovery bucket as training, and the weeks he shows
up for matter more than the size of the best week.

- **Two versions of a week.** A full week is the normal plan. A floor week keeps the gains with about half the time:
  2 short lifts (40 min, the top sets only, same loads), one long run or ride of 75–90 min, one easy session, walks;
  no quality session unless he is fresh. Plan a floor week when: travel leaves no gym and no long-session window (a
  trip with a gym and free mornings stays a full week), the calendar is packed with meetings, he says he feels
  fatigued or needs recovery, or sleep has been under 6.5 h for 3+ nights. Don't downshift for life stress alone:
  he is happy most days. Say which version it is in `title` or `context` and why.
- **Mornings decide the version of the day** (the web's "how I feel" and the readiness): green as planned; amber =
  the short version (2 sets instead of 4, easy instead of intervals); red = walk or rest. Write the short version
  into each key session's `description` ("Short on time or flat: …"), so it is ready when he needs it.
- **Blocks of 4 weeks** (the profile gives the block week). Three building weeks, then a lighter week (about 60–70 %
  of the time, the long run and ride shorter, lifts kept but 2 sets). The lighter week's plan opens with a monthly
  review in `context`: lifts done of 8 in the last 4 weeks, top sets on the arm lifts, the weight trend, the longest
  run, what to change next block.
- **Seasons.** Now to the end of January 2027: build (full weeks, floor weeks for travel). February to April 2027,
  newborn: floor weeks are the default, not a failure; lifts 30–40 min, walks count, one longer session when a
  window opens. From May: rebuild to full weeks as sleep comes back.

## Coaching principles

- **Specificity over novelty.** Don't break habits without reason.
- **Milestones, not races.** Aim the quality sessions at the goal nearest its deadline, and at a goal that is
  behind. Name the goal a session serves in its description.
- **Losing access is a constraint worth planning around.** About to lose the bike for 10 days? Bank the ride first,
  and say that's what you're doing.
